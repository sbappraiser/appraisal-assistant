How to

Step-by-step directions for each part of the Appraisal Assistant. Type a word to find the right section.

The octopus – how the Appraisal Assistant is laid out
  1. Top right, always there: Home · Library · Comments (your comment library) · Database center · Settings (data folder, password lock, Claude) · Help.
  2. The blue bar is the open order, tentacle by tentacle, in the order you work: 1 Intake (this order's documents, engagement letter, assignment and scope; also All orders, New order, Clients, Bookkeeping, Letter templates) · 2 Subject (inspection and subject details) · 3 Market (market tab, then comp selection) · 4 Sales comparison (comp data, grid, support, comment) · 5 Cost · 6 Income (only when you develop it) · 7 Reconciliation · 8 Review and wrap-up (review genie, pre-check, SFREP export) · 9 Exhibits (maps, photos).
  3. The Comp genie is the help for the four tentacles that use data, databases and comparables – 3 Market, 4 Sales comparison, 5 Cost, 6 Income (purple underline). 2 Subject, 7 Reconciliation, 8 Review and wrap-up and 9 Exhibits are tentacles of the Appraisal Assistant. The Head (top right) holds the tools for every section: Library, Common responses, Databases, Claude, Settings, Help.
  4. A green ✓ on a tentacle means everything in it is done. Tentacles 2 and 4–9 open inside this window; their numbered steps underneath carry the same tentacle number.
  5. Click a tentacle with no order open and it asks you to open one first (1 Intake › All orders, or New order).
  6. Being built next: each answer in one home only (tentacle 2 for highest and best use, assumptions and conditions), then the Library and the Database center. The plan is your doc “The Octopus – where everything lives”.
Start a new order
  1. On Home, click New order.
  2. 1 · Order: type the street address only (with the unit, e.g. #3), then Tab to City, State, ZIP and +4. City and ZIP are drop-down lists of the places you work (Santa Barbara County first, then Ventura County) – click and scroll with the mouse wheel. Not in the list? Pick Other – type it. Picking a ZIP fills the city, and Goleta, Carpinteria, Summerland or Montecito fill their ZIP; you can change either. When you upload the CRS report, it puts in the USPS form of the address with the ZIP+4. The file number fills in by your rule: the first 4 consonants of the street name (short names like Bath or Hill keep their first 4 letters), the house number, the unit if any, and the report count. 1729 Bath St → Bath1729-1; unit B → Bath1729-b-1; 223 W. Arrellaga St #3 → Arrl223-3-1. If you have done this property before in the assistant (or an old order is numbered the ACI way, like Bath1729a or Bath1729b), the count goes up: Bath1729-2. You can always type over it. Answer three questions: did the client send an engagement letter or will you write one, is it a purchase, and is the property listed now or in the last 12 months. Click Next.
  3. 2 · Documents: drop each file in its box – client’s engagement letter, CRS report, legal description, MLS listing with history, purchase contract with counters and addenda (as many PDFs as you have), preliminary title report, and anything else under Other documents. Each file is read as you add it, and checked against the property address: a file for another property is marked ⚠ and nothing is filled in from it.
  4. 3 · Check: see what was filled in. The client’s letter sets the client, loan number, borrower, due date, fee, report form, rent schedule, and AI use: a ban (for example “no AI without prior authorization”) sets No AI; confidentiality wording sets Yes – only with confidential information removed; nothing sets Yes. You can change any of it on the order.
  5. Click Read next to the prelim or contract to summarize it, then Open the order – or, if you are writing the letter, Next: write my engagement letter.
  6. The order saves by itself. To find it again, go to Home: Open an order lists your last 10 orders (click a row to open it); All files lets you search by file number, address and/or borrower – fill in any of the boxes and click Search (leave them empty to list every order).
  7. To delete an order from either list, click Delete at the end of its row, then click it again (it turns red and says Click again to delete) within 3 seconds. The order and all its uploaded documents are removed from this computer – make a Back up first if you might want it later.
Report form format and form
  1. Report form format: in New order step 1, click UAD 3.6 (Traditional), UAD 2.6 (Legacy), GPAR or Other. You can change it later in Scope and report on the order page.
  2. Form (just below it on the order page): the assistant picks the usual one for the property type and inspection – e.g. UAD 2.6 condo → Form 1073, exterior-only → Form 2055. Click it to choose another (1004, 1073, 1025, 2055, 1075, 1004C, 2090, 2095, 1004 Desktop, 1004D; URAR kinds for 3.6; GPAR forms), or Other – type it. Once you choose, it stays. It is the "Format" line in your engagement letter.
  3. Your choices are kept – the assistant does not change them, even if the client's letter names a different form. If you have not chosen, a letter that names a 1004 / 1073 / 1025 sets UAD 2.6 and that form.
Permits: read all of them together with Claude (one timeline)
  1. Put all the permit files for the property in one folder on your computer. In Documents click Choose a folder. The window only shows folders, so your Permits folder looks empty inside – that is normal. Click the Permits folder once (do not open it), click Upload (or Select Folder), and when Chrome asks “Upload 6 files to this site?” click Upload. Nothing leaves your computer – the files stay in this browser. Or drag the whole folder onto the box. Every file inside is added; files in a folder named Permits are filed as Permit records.
  2. Under the document list, click Read all the permits together with Claude. You first see what will be sent: each file, how many pages go as text and how many as pictures (scans), and small pictures of the scanned pages. Click Send … pages to Claude.
  3. All the files go in one request, so Claude can match the same permit across files and build one timeline: permit number, work, square feet, applied / issued / final dates and status, with the file and page. Lots of scanned pages are read in parts and then put together.
  4. Under To check Claude lists permits never finaled or expired, added or converted living area compared with the public record, code-enforcement cases, and work for another address. A draft permit comment is offered – read and edit it; nothing goes into the report by itself.
  5. To check is your to-do list, not something to click: settle each item from what you know, your inspection or your sketch, and put the answer in the comment. The draft permit comment is written your way: the current owner's work newest first ("the current owner completed a garage re-roof in 2025 (25CNP-00019)…"), then "The owner reported recent upgrades include: ___ (based on interview)" for you to fill in, then work by prior owners. Open questions and enforcement cases are left to To check, not the comment. Click Read all the permits again to get a comment in the new wording.
  6. If you drag a permit folder and only a 1 KB item called "permits" appears, that is a Windows shortcut, not the PDFs: remove it, open the folder, press Ctrl+A and drag the files in.
  7. The permit box shows the same tags as the documents: scan – needs Claude or Acrobat (brown) before it is read, then ✓ read by Claude and ✓ done · N permits in the timeline (solid green). Each permit file also shows ✓ done · in the permit timeline. If you add a permit file later, a brown new file not read yet tag tells you to read them all again.
  8. This only works when the order's AI use is Yes or Redacted. On Redacted orders names, phones and emails are removed from the text (permit numbers stay); scanned pages are only sent from a redacted copy you made (Read › Redacted copy).
Emails in the workfile (Outlook)
  1. In Outlook, drag the email out of the message list onto the order's Documents box (classic Outlook makes a .msg file). In the new Outlook or Outlook on the web, open the email, choose … › Save as (or Download) to save a .eml file, then drop that file in Documents. Several at once is fine.
  2. It is filed as Email: the note shows who sent it, the date and the subject; click the name to read it. Its words are found by Search every document.
  3. Every attachment is added to the order as its own document – sorted by kind and read like any other upload (an engagement letter, a permit, a contract) – with “from the email …” in its note. Small pictures such as signature logos are left out.
  4. It is all kept in this browser on this computer, with the order (and in Back up). Nothing is sent anywhere.

Documents: upload once, kinds, search, sticky notes
  1. Which documents fill things in by themselves? Each one shows a tag next to its name (point at it for more): ✓ read here – read when you uploaded it (CRS, MLS, County permit history, the client's letter, your signed letter, floor plan); what it filled in is listed after the size. ✓ text – click Read – the text can be read here, but nothing is filled in until you click Read and accept the suggestions (contract, prelim, deed, legal description, HOA papers). scan – needs Claude or Acrobat – a picture of the pages; click Read to send it to Claude (only when the order's AI use allows it), or in Acrobat use Scan & OCR › Recognize text, save, and upload it again. part scan – some pages are pictures. kept, not read – photos and other files are stored with the order only.
  2. After a document is read, its tag changes so you never do it twice: ✓ read by Claude (green outline) once Claude has read it – also for permit files read together; 3 to accept or reject (orange) while suggestions are waiting; and ✓ done · 2 accepted · filled in: Legal description (solid green) when every suggestion is accepted or rejected, naming what it filled in on the order.
  3. Uploading a file with the same name as one already in the order replaces it – you never get two copies. Its sticky notes and accepted entries stay. (Orders that already had a file twice are cleaned up when you open them.)
  4. Drop every file for the order into the Documents box, or click Choose files. Each one is sorted by kind; change the kind with its drop-down if it guessed wrong.
  5. Click a file name to open it. Click Read to open the reader (next section). Remove takes it out of the order.
  6. Search: type a word in "Search every document…" and press Enter or Search. It looks through the text of every document, your sticky notes and your accepted entries. Click a result to open that page with the word highlighted.
  7. Scanned PDFs have no text to search. Open the file in Acrobat → Scan & OCR → Recognize text, save, and drop it in again.
  8. Sticky notes are listed under Documents. Use the drop-down to show one tag (for example "Clear before transfer"). Click one to jump to its page.
  9. Your signed engagement letter: drop it in Documents (it is recognized as My engagement letter (signed)). The order then follows the letter: date of agreement, assignment type (e.g. Refinance), client name/company, address, phone and email, the access contact (when it is not "Client (see above)"), APN, fee and retainer – and the order is marked Signed. The file's note lists what changed. Orders that already had your letter uploaded are read once the next time you open them. A scanned letter cannot be read – check those boxes yourself.
  10. Deeds (grant deed, trust transfer deed, quitclaim): choose Deed in the drop-down (it is picked by itself when page 1 says so). Click Read: it suggests the legal description, vesting, reservations (oil, gas, minerals) and the kind of deed. Accept the legal description and it goes into the order's Legal description box (used by the Comp genie and SFREP). A legal description accepted from a Legal description or prelim document does the same. Accept a reservation (for example "excepting all oil, gas and mineral rights below 500 feet") and a short version goes into the order's Rights not appraised box (540 characters, the UAD 3.6 limit). It only reaches the report if you answer No to "All rights included in the appraisal?" in the Comp genie's report section – that is your call.
  11. Any scanned document (deed, signed letter, permit): the reader says the page is a scan. On an order where AI use is Yes or Redacted and your Claude key is saved, it shows Send the scanned pages to Claude right there – you see the pictures before they go. Otherwise it tells you what to change.
  12. Scanned deeds have no text to read. Either open the PDF in Acrobat → Scan & OCR → Recognize text, save, and drop it here again; or, on an order where AI is allowed, use Read with Claude; or type it in the reader's box under Legal description.
Read a document: checklist, accept or reject, sticky notes
  1. In Documents, click Read. The page is on the left; the checklist for that kind of document is on the right. Use Previous / Next or the arrow keys to turn pages.
  2. Suggestions (from the text, or from Claude) appear in blue boxes with their page. Edit the words if needed, then Accept or Reject. Nothing goes into the order until you accept it.
  3. To add your own entry, read the page, type in "Type it yourself" and click Add. It keeps the page number.
  4. Sticky note: click + Sticky note on page …, pick a tag (Important, Affects market appeal, Clear before transfer, Ask the client, Put in the report, Follow up), type the note, Save note. Tick done when it is handled.
  5. Click Done to close the reader. Accepted entries are listed under Documents.
Preliminary title report
  1. Read the prelim. The date and the exceptions worth mentioning are suggested for you: easements, restrictions and CC&Rs, oil/gas/mineral items, encroachments, and items that may need to be cleared before a transfer. Deeds of trust, trust requirements, general printed items and paid taxes are left out (delinquent taxes are kept).
  2. Accept the ones you want. Under UAD 3.6 site – encumbrances, answer the easement, restriction, encroachment and mineral questions.
  3. Title summary for the report is drafted from what you accepted, with your title-expert statement. Decide how each easement or restriction affects market appeal, edit, then Copy and paste into SFREP.
Purchase contract packet
  1. Read the contract. C.A.R. forms print in scrambled text, so fill the checklist yourself while you read, or use Read with Claude on an AI order.
  2. Checklist: final negotiated price, acceptance date, personal property, leaseback, credits, buyer's broker (who pays, % or $), counters and addenda, loan and appraisal contingency, signing for an entity, court-ordered/estate/trust/atypical terms, written-in terms.
  3. Parts of this document: go to the first page of each part and click + Add a part; set its kind and pages. The sentence "I received a 75-page document which contains…" is built for you.
  4. When the contract has readable text (typed or run through Acrobat's Recognize Text), the reader suggests the price, acceptance date, close of escrow, credits, buyer's broker compensation, personal property, leaseback, 1031 exchange, appraisal and loan contingency, and estate/trust/court terms, each with its page. On C.A.R. forms only the typed-in entries can be read, so it offers the largest typed amount on the first pages as the price – check it against the counters.
  5. Accept, edit then Accept, or Reject each suggestion. An accepted price, acceptance date, personal property or credit also fills the empty UAD 3.6 sales contract boxes, which the Comp genie puts in the report.
  6. Check the UAD 3.6 sales contract boxes. 20.011 Sales contract analysis is drafted from your entries; edit, Copy, paste into SFREP.
  7. 20.011 Sales contract analysis starts as a draft in your own layout (ANALYSIS OF THE SALES CONTRACT): document and page count, price and acceptance date, seller and signer, contingencies, close of escrow, personal property included and excluded (new checklist line), leaseback, 1031, buyer's broker, credits, "All other terms … fairly typical", no counter offers, and your furnishings paragraph when furnishings transfer. [Brackets] are for you – arm's length, seller and signer names, effect on price. Redo rebuilds it from the facts you accepted.

My comments – your comment library
  1. Click the My comments tab.
  2. First time: under Import my OneNote library, choose what the comments were written for (your OneNote library is UAD 2.6), then click Choose the PDF or comment file… and pick the PDF you exported from OneNote (or a comment file Claude made for you – those carry their own UAD 2.6 / 3.6 tag). Each page becomes one comment, sorted by its OneNote section; long comments that run onto a second page are joined back together. Pages with a title but no text yet are left out and listed.
  3. To use one: type a word in the search box (for example laser, ADU, fire) or pick a section, then click Copy and paste it into SFREP. Blanks in square brackets, like [date], are highlighted so you remember to fill them in.
  4. The tag on each comment shows the form it was written for: UAD 2.6, UAD 3.6 or 2.6 and 3.6. Use the drop-down at the top to show only the ones for the form you are working on. When you have checked a 2.6 comment for 3.6, click Edit and change Written for.
  5. Edit changes the title, section, form or wording; Delete (click twice) removes it. Add a comment starts a new one (marked UAD 3.6).
  6. Comments from your template are grouped by the UAD 3.6 box they go in (Scope of work, Market, Site, Hazard zones, Reconciliation, Cost approach …). The heading shows how many characters that box holds – everything you paste into it together – and each comment shows its own length. Where your full text is longer than the box, a condensed version that fits is included (its title starts with the box name in capitals, e.g. MARKET, COST APPROACH).
  7. Importing the same OneNote PDF again later adds the new comments and updates the ones you have not edited; your edited ones stay as you have them.
  8. Every comment box – here (Scope of work, highest and best use, extraordinary assumptions, market…) and in the Comp genie – knows its section. Click into the box: you can type, or click one of the comment names that appear under it to put that comment in where your cursor is.
  9. 📋 My comments under a box opens all of that section's comments with the full wording: Use this (empty box), Add it (adds at your cursor) or Replace my text. Tick show every section to look further, or search. UAD 2.6 comments stay hidden unless you tick them.
  10. The My comments tab shows a small box for each part of the report, in report order (Assignment and scope of work, Subject, Market, Site, Highest and best use, Improvements, Sales contract, Sales comparison, Cost, Income, Reconciliation, Certifications). Click a box to see only its comments; click it again to close it. Searching looks in every section and shows how many were found in each box.
  11. Which comments show for a box goes by the Section name you gave each comment (e.g. "Highest and best use commentary"). If a comment doesn't show where you expect, Edit it here and change its Section.
  12. Additional appraiser certifications (e.g. your Appraisal Institute ones): Comp genie › Report details › Prior services and certifications › answer Yes. One certification per box (up to 6, 360 characters each – SFREP numbers each one). Use my usual certifications fills them; after you edit them, Save these as my usual and they are the ones used next time.
  13. Extraordinary assumptions and hypothetical conditions have no separate SFREP field: your wording goes in Additional Scope of Work, and the Market value condition (As is / Subject to repair / Subject to inspection / Subject to completion per plans) tells Fannie Mae one was used. The pre-check warns when they don't agree – e.g. an extraordinary assumption with "As is".
  14. SFREP has three scope of work places: the Scope of Work paragraph is printed by Fannie Mae and cannot be changed; Additional Scope of Work is the order's Scope of work box (what you did beyond the standard) together with your extraordinary assumptions and hypothetical conditions – 2,500 characters for all three; Assignment Information and Scope of Work Commentary (e.g. your inspection-limits paragraph) is the last box under Assignment in the Comp genie's Report details.
  15. The Comp genie's path now runs: ④ Inspection → ⑤ Assignment, scope and site (assignment, scope of work, client-requested conditions, extraordinary assumptions and hypothetical conditions, highest and best use, subject and site with the site and subject comments, prior services and certifications) → ⑥ Market → comps, grid and support → ⑫ Cost → ⑬ Income (its own page; only when you develop it, e.g. with a rent survey – answer Yes to "Income approach developed?") → ⑭ Reconciliation (the transfer-history analyses, each approach's indicated value filled in, the opinion of value – it starts as the sales comparison value until you type yours – exposure time and the reconciliation comment) → ⑮ Exhibits → ⑯ Review and export.
  16. In the Comp genie, each line of a red “answers still needed” list is a link: click it to go to that box. Legal description: Import the deed (PDF) beside the box reads the legal description (and an EXCEPTING/RESERVING clause, e.g. mineral rights, into Rights not included). A scanned deed with no text needs Acrobat › Scan & OCR › Recognize text first. Check the result – scanned text can misread a character (e.g. “S00 FEET” for 500).
  17. Each comment box shows how many characters you have used of what SFREP allows (e.g. 412 / 5,000) as you type; it turns red at the limit.
  18. Market commentary (what the numbers don't show) is on the order's Market tab under the Price Trend commentary, with your other market answers. Additional client-requested conditions are on the order's ③ Scope of work tab; the Comp genie uses them unless you change them there.
  19. Exposure time: the days-on-market table in Reconciliation now shows each comp's list price, sale price and sale-to-list ratio, and how many sold over the list price.
  20. Draft from your grid: in the Comp genie, under the sales comparison comment, click Draft the comment from my grid. It writes the comparable paragraphs (superior / inferior overall, each adjustment with its amount, your low / mid / high tiers, sale-to-list price, your notes for the comp in [brackets]), your rate paragraphs with the rates on the grid, and the start of the reconciliation (bracketing and adjusted range, your weights). Read it, fill in the [brackets], then click Put it in the comment or Add it to the end. Nothing goes in until you click.
  21. Your library is kept in this browser on this computer and in your Back up zip – keep backing up.
Invoice, payment and the unpaid list (Bookkeeping tab)
  1. Everything about the money is on the Bookkeeping tab: this order's invoice and payment at the top, then every unpaid invoice and the half-year summary.
  2. The invoice number is your file number with -inv (brkl5464 → brkl5464-inv). The invoice date is the day you sign and deliver the report: set the order status to Delivered (or type Report signed and delivered) and both are filled in. Change either if you need to.
  3. The invoice is not part of the UAD 3.6 report file, so it does not go into SFREP: send the invoice PDF with the report (email or the client's portal).
  4. The fee comes from Total fee (Fee and delivery). A rent survey fee is added when the rent survey is ticked; a retainer is taken off the balance.
  5. When you deliver the report, go to the Bookkeeping tab and click Make the invoice. It fills in the invoice number (file number + -inv), the delivered date (today if blank) and the amount (type a different amount to change it). A new tab opens with the invoice on your letterhead: in the print window choose Destination: Save as PDF, then Save. Attach that PDF to your email or the AMC portal. AMC orders are billed to the AMC, with the AMC order / reference ID (for example the Document ID) on the invoice.
  6. When the money comes in, click Mark paid today (or type the date), pick how it was paid and the amount. The order status changes to Paid.
  7. Home shows Unpaid invoices with the days outstanding (over 30 days in red). Follow-up email opens a polite reminder in your mail program – read it, change it if you like, and send it yourself. Nothing is sent by the assistant. Orders marked Delivered with no invoice are listed under it.
  8. Twice a year, pick the half year under the list and click Print the summary: invoices dated in that half year, retainers, payments received, and what is still unpaid. Save it as a PDF for your bookkeeper.
  9. Invoices and payments are part of each order, so Back up keeps them.

  2. On the right, find Redact a copy. Set the pages (all pages are filled in) and click Redact pages.
  3. Names from the order, emails, phone numbers, DocuSign IDs and loan/case/escrow numbers are blacked out for you. Type any other names (buyers, sellers, agents) in Also remove these names, separated by commas, then click outside the box.
  4. Choose Black box or Blur, then drag over signatures, initials, account numbers or anything else. Undo last box takes back the last one.
  5. Check every page, then click Save redacted PDF. It saves to Downloads (name ends in "REDACTED") and is added to the order's documents. The copy is pictures only, with no hidden text, so nothing removed can be recovered.

  3. From then on a backup is saved there by itself every day you work (about 1½ minutes after you save, and when you leave the page) – orders, clients, documents, your lists and your Comp genie assignments. Today’s file is replaced as you work; the last 30 days are kept.
